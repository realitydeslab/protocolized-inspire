# Protocolized Inspire — catalog

A catalog of protocol art: works in which an artist or designer writes a protocol — instructions, a score, a contract, a game, a smart contract — that shapes what a group of people does, and that group behaviour is the work. From 1920s instructions and Fluxus scores to smart-contract art and DAOs, compiled by Reality Design Lab as idea material. Each work lists its core idea, its rules, what the collective did, and links to images, video, texts and contracts.

## Social Protocols & Economies

Rules for groups, exchanges and contracts in physical space: social sculpture, performance protocols, mail-art networks and artist-made economies.

### Contracts, Currencies & Exchange

Artist-designed contracts, receipts, currencies and exchange rituals: the pre-blockchain ancestors of tokens and royalties.

#### Self Checkout — Jack Butcher (2025)
- Type: Artwork · Substrate: Bodies & space · Mechanisms: Market & exchange, Open participation, Currency & value
- Idea: Take the jibe that an NFT is 'just a receipt' literally, and make every payment lengthen the work and move a public balance.
- What it is: A retail checkout installed at Art Basel Miami Beach (Zero 10, December 2025): visitors pay what they choose and receive a thermal receipt, which is the artwork, while a split-flap display shows the artist's running balance.
- Protocol: Three in-person kiosks and one kiosk for remote orders accept any amount; each dollar adds length to the printed receipt. Each receipt carries a printed seed phrase giving access to a non-transferable digital companion on Ethereum. Online and in-person payments feed the same displayed balance, which started from the artist's own exhibition costs.
- What the collective did: Fair visitors and remote buyers paid varying amounts, lined up for signatures and pushed the displayed balance; press reports gave interim figures only, so no final total is recorded here.

#### Work, Luck, Play — Jack Butcher (2026)
- Type: Game & experiment · Substrate: Bodies & space · Mechanisms: Market & exchange, Chance & randomness, Circulation & passing on
- Idea: Separate effort, fixed luck and choices made together: the finished set can only come from trading with strangers.
- What it is: Silver sculptures and a trading game presented by Silk at Art Basel Hong Kong (March 2026): a dice roll gave each participant six resin dice, and participants traded with each other to complete a set of one to six.
- Protocol: A roll of ordinary dice decided which six resin dice a visitor received (Luck's dice carry one number on every face). Participants could trade to assemble 1 through 6 and return for a signed set, or keep an incomplete combination. An online rolling system on Ethereum and a token-burning route to claim a silver die ran alongside.
- What the collective did: Fair visitors traded dice among themselves to complete sets, and an accumulating archive ('Luckiest') recorded the rolls; no participant counts have been published.
- Images: https://jack.art/play/wlp-thumb.png https://jack.art/play/play-dice.webp
- Project page: https://jack.art/work-luck-play

## Smart-Contract Art

Artworks whose rules run in a smart contract: supply, burning, merging, transfer, taxes and co-authorship enforced on-chain, and shaped by what collectors do.

### Supply, Burn & Merge

Open editions, burning, merging and composites: the final form is set by how many people buy, burn or combine.

#### Merge — Pak (2021)
- Type: Artwork · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Merge & composite, Supply & scarcity, Circulation & passing on
- Idea: A game of extinction: a contract where every transfer can consume a token, so the market itself shrinks the work toward one survivor.
- What it is: An open sale of 'mass' on Nifty Gateway (2–4 December 2021). Each buyer's units became one dynamic token, and any two tokens that meet in the same wallet fuse, so the number of tokens only goes down.
- Protocol: During the 48-hour sale anyone could buy any number of mass units; at the end each collector's units were merged into one token. The contract lets an ordinary wallet hold only one token: when a second arrives, the larger mass absorbs the smaller and the smaller is burned (whitelisted addresses such as the Nifty Gateway omnibus are exempt). Each token has one of four classes, drawn as a white, yellow, blue-ringed or red-ringed sphere; the single largest mass is the 'alpha', drawn black with a white ring, and every sphere is sized relative to it.
- What the collective did: 28,983 collectors spent $91,806,519 on 312,686 units of mass, which became one token each (Nifty Gateway / Gemini). Secondary trading and deliberate merging then consumed tokens: by September 2026 the contract's totalSupply was 18,961, held by about 9,635 addresses (Blockscout).

#### Rocket Factory — Tom Sachs (2021)
- Type: Series & collection · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Merge & composite, Burn & destruction, Chance & randomness
- Idea: The collector base, not the artist, decides which of 113,000+ possible rockets get built, and the studio has to follow.
- What it is: 3,000 NFT rocket parts (nose cones, bodies, tail assemblies) in 30 brand liveries. Collectors combine three parts into a rocket, and may ask the studio to build and launch a physical twin.
- Protocol: One nose cone, one body and one tail assembly can be combined into a Completed Rocket NFT; the three component NFTs are burned ('comburning'). Same-brand parts make a Perfect Rocket, mixed parts a Frankenrocket. A rocket holder can choose the Launch Option, after which the studio builds and launches a physical rocket and returns recovered parts in a display box with launch video; the physical twin can also be donated or destroyed.
- What the collective did: Collectors trade parts to chase scarce Perfect Rockets (for example 87 Chanel and 3 Hello Kitty sets exist). By the 2022 Wallpaper* Design Awards there had been seven public launches, including Governors Island in New York, a Miami ballpark and SoHo; LACMA joined with a collaborative Frankenrocket.

#### The Currency — Damien Hirst (2021)
- Type: Series & collection · Substrate: Other chains · Mechanisms: Burn & destruction, Contract & certificate, Market & exchange
- Idea: Make every owner choose between the token and the object, and let the sum of those choices decide how much of the work physically survives.
- What it is: 10,000 NFTs, each tied to a unique hand-painted dot work on paper, released with HENI in July 2021. Each buyer had a year to keep the NFT or swap it for the painting; whichever was not chosen was destroyed.
- Protocol: Holders of each NFT (on the Palm blockchain) could, until 27 July 2022, exchange it for the matching paper work, which burned the NFT; if they kept the NFT, the paper work would be burned. Hirst kept 1,000 works himself and chose the NFT for them, so their papers burned too.
- What the collective did: When the exchange period closed, 5,149 holders had chosen the physical work and 4,851 the NFT (HENI). The 4,851 papers were burned publicly during The Currency exhibition at Newport Street Gallery, London, from September to October 2022, starting on 11 October with Hirst's own 1,000.

#### The Fungible — Pak (2021)
- Type: Series & collection · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Supply & scarcity, Merge & composite, Market & exchange
- Idea: Sell a fungible unit and let the quantity each person buys decide which non-fungible artwork they end up holding.
- What it is: Pak's sale with Sotheby's on Nifty Gateway (12–14 April 2021): identical 'cubes' were sold as an open edition for 15 minutes a day, and each buyer's cubes were then packed into NFTs whose image depends on how many they bought.
- Protocol: Anyone could buy any number of cubes during a 15-minute window each day, at $500, then $1,000, then $1,500. After the sale each buyer's total was split into the largest possible packs: A Cube (1), Five, Ten, Twenty, Fifty, Hundred, Five Hundred and Thousand Cubes (six cubes = one Five Cubes + one A Cube). The top buyer received The Cube, the next 100 buyers received Complexity, and Equilibrium went to four winners of set challenges (puzzle, highest secondary purchase, largest #PakWasHere post, closest estimate of the total).
- What the collective did: Buyers took 19,737 cubes on day one, 3,268 on day two and 593 on day three: 23,598 cubes for about $14 million. With the two auctioned 1/1s (The Switch, The Pixel) the sale reached $16.8 million (Sotheby's). Many cube holders later burned their cubes on burn.art to mine ASH.

#### burn.art and $ASH — Pak (2021)
- Type: Platform & protocol · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Burn & destruction, Currency & value, Supply & scarcity
- Idea: A currency backed by what its holders have destroyed: every burn makes the remaining works rarer.
- What it is: A site where anyone can destroy an NFT and receive ASH, an ERC-20 token that started with zero supply and can only be mined by burning. ASH then became the only currency accepted for some Pak drops.
- Protocol: Any holder can burn an NFT on burn.art and receive newly minted ASH. The yield follows a bonding curve that falls as supply grows, with two tiers: Pak NFTs start at 1,000 ASH, all other NFTs at 2 ASH; a halving hits every tier at 5 million ASH. Liquidity pools are left to the community. From January 2022 (Chapter One: Carbon) some Pak drops could only be bought or bid on with ASH.
- What the collective did: Collectors burned their own and others' NFTs, above all Fungible cubes, to mine ASH; Carbon's single edition is titled 'Three Thousand Eight Hundred Twenty Five Burned Cubes', and only ASH holders could bid on it. By September 2026 the ASH contract showed about 3.54 million tokens held by about 9,950 addresses (Blockscout).

#### Matter* (mass.black) — Pak (2022)
- Type: Game & experiment · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Merge & composite, Rules & constraints, Chance & randomness
- Idea: Reward specific collective behaviours (merging often, hoarding mass, cannibalising) so holders play the contract as a game.
- What it is: A month-long 'treasure hunt' for Merge holders (1–30 April 2022): new Matter* tokens were awarded to Merge tokens that met published criteria about merges and mass at the end snapshot.
- Protocol: Tiers of Matter* went to Merge tokens by rule: 'Hunters and Traps' for tokens with 4+ merges, 'Thief's Wise Hands' for mass × merges ≥ 100 (stealing supply from 'Scavenger's Weak Hands', mass ≥ 10), 'Cannibalism' for the most merges, 'Kings of the Hill' for the most mass, 'Titan' for the alpha, 'Three Lucky Giants' at random, and a hidden challenge tier that would unlock only if Merge supply fell below 20,000. Distribution used the end snapshot; one token could win several.
- What the collective did: The site reports a Matter* supply of 1,481 with 1,395 generated. The 'Thief' tier took all 450 places, so 'Scavenger's Weak Hands' generated none, and the challenge tier stayed locked because Merge supply did not fall below 20,000 during the event.

#### Checks — Jack Butcher (2023)
- Type: Series & collection · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Burn & destruction, Merge & composite, Supply & scarcity
- Idea: Collectors decide which images survive: every composite destroys a work, and choosing not to act is also part of the piece.
- What it is: An $8 open edition of 80 checkmarks (January 2023), after Twitter began selling verification. From February 2023 collectors could destroy an edition to get an on-chain Original, then burn Originals into composites with fewer and fewer checks.
- Protocol: Checks – VV Edition was open for 24 hours at $8. Any holder may, with no deadline, burn an Edition to mint an Original with the same token number, rendered as an animated SVG from on-chain data. Two Originals with the same check count can be composited into one: 80 → 40 → 20 → 10 → 5 → 4 → 1 check; the holder picks which token number survives and the parents set the colours. At the last stage 64 single checks make a Black Check, the sum of 4,096 original editions, so at most three can ever exist.
- What the collective did: 16,031 editions were minted in the first 24 hours. By September 2026 about 3,560 Editions had never been migrated and the Originals contract held 5,347 surviving works, so collectors have burned thousands of tokens through migration and compositing (Blockscout).

#### 1 of 1 of ? — Jack Butcher (2024)

#### Full Set — Jack Butcher (2025)
- Type: Series & collection · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Supply & scarcity, Open participation, Rules & constraints
- Idea: A single collecting threshold that can rewrite, or undo, what earlier buyers own.
- What it is: Twenty-seven editions on an Ethereum layer 2 (with Rodeo). The first 26 images were replaced by one shared grid, and the 27th edition decided whether that change would last.
- Protocol: On 10 January 2025 Butcher announced that if token 27 sold more than 159,300 editions (the combined count of the first 26), the network grid would remain the artwork; otherwise the first 26 would revert to their original images.
- What the collective did: Buyers of token 27 decided the outcome for holders of all earlier works. A progress post followed on 11 January, and the studio catalogue reports that the threshold was passed (the crossing transaction has not been published).

#### Credits — Jack Butcher (2026)
- Type: Series & collection · Substrate: Internet & platforms · Mechanisms: Burn & destruction, Merge & composite, Currency & value
- Idea: Payment records as printing plates: collectors spend trust (credits) to make fewer, denser prints.
- What it is: Each eligible $8 payment through X Money becomes a Credit, an image of four 8 × 8 CMYK grids derived from the transaction ID. Holders can burn 80 Credits to compose a Statement and overprint Statements onto each other.
- Protocol: A transaction ID is hashed (SHA-256) into 256 bits, split into cyan, magenta, yellow and black 64-bit grids; the timestamp selects which layers show. Burning 80 Credits composes a Statement, one Credit per cell. Overprinting one Statement onto another burns the top one and multiplies the inks. Statements are capped at 1,526 and the minimum is 1.
- What the collective did: Credits are being issued from X Money payments; Statement assembly opens on 1 October 2026, so how far collectors will compress the supply is not yet known.

### Ownership & Transfer Rules

Contracts that change what owning means: always for sale, must be passed on, dies if held, changes with its owner.

#### Economy of Words — Jack Butcher (2026)
- Type: Series & collection · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Market & exchange, Supply & scarcity, Contract & certificate
- Idea: Three hierarchies compete over the same words: a mechanical grade, what bidders pay, and the material form each winner picks.
- What it is: A card for each of the 2,048 words of the BIP39 seed-phrase list, each graded by a formula and sold in its own auction; winners then chose whether the card would exist as a physical graded card or as a synthetic card on Ethereum.
- Protocol: Grades (10 bands) come from word length, initial letter, letter values and list position. Each auction opened at $1 and ran 20 hours 48 minutes, extended by late bids. Winners had 2,048 minutes after closing to choose physical or synthetic; the choice then locked.
- What the collective did: The studio's collector board ranks 331 holders of the 2,048 words by spend and shows the physical/synthetic split; final totals are not yet published.
- Images: https://jack.art/eow-hero.webp https://jack.art/eow-ui-collectors.webp
- Project page: https://jack.art/economy-of-words

### Programmable & Co-authored Works

Works whose layers, parameters or worlds are written by their holders and by the community that builds on them.

#### Lost Poets — Pak (2021)
- Type: Game & experiment · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Burn & destruction, Co-authorship & derivatives, Chance & randomness
- Idea: Give buyers a fuel (Pages) that can only be spent by destroying it, and let them spend it on authoring the characters they own.
- What it is: A collectible strategy game in acts: 65,536 Pages were sold in September 2021, holders burned Pages to claim AI-generated Poets, then burned more Pages to name their Poets and give them words.
- Protocol: Act I sold Pages (an ERC-1155 token) at 0.32 ETH, with 7,586 Pages airdropped to holders of at least 25 ASH. For two weeks one Page could be burned to claim one Poet (ERC-721); after that no more conversions were possible. 1,024 Origin Poets with special permissions were distributed at random. Burning further Pages into a Poet grants a name change and a few words that the holder can write into the Poet's metadata.
- What the collective did: All 65,536 Pages sold in about two hours of a planned 48-hour sale. Holders then named their Poets and inscribed short lines (on-chain names include 'The Last Poet' and 'Pope Francis'), so the collection's texts were written by its owners. In September 2026 the Poets contract listed about 5,570 holders (Blockscout).

#### Censored — Pak (2022)
- Type: Series & collection · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Co-authorship & derivatives, Open participation, Time, decay & death
- Idea: Every buyer writes part of the work, and a real-world event (a prisoner's release) is the key that reveals it.
- What it is: An open edition made with Julian Assange (February 2022): for 48 hours anyone could mint a token carrying a message of their own, shown under a black censor bar until Assange was freed.

#### Opepen Edition — Jack Butcher (2023)
- Type: Platform & protocol · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Voting & governance, Co-authorship & derivatives, Chance & randomness
- Idea: Holders do not vote on proposals; they commit their own token to the image it will become.
- What it is: 16,000 identical tokens (a geometric Pepe) that became a release system: artists propose sets of images, and holders opt their unrevealed tokens into the sets they want to become.
- Protocol: Opepen was a 60-minute open edition (8 January 2023). Tokens are revealed in sets of 80 with edition groups of 1, 4, 5, 10, 20 and 40. - What the collective did: Set 001 (8x8, by Butcher) was submitted on 30 April 2023 and reached consensus on 3 May 2023; later sets came from many artists. In September 2026 the contract counted 15,766 tokens held by about 8,500 addresses (Blockscout).

### Early Tokens & Provenance

The first crypto-art tokens, registries and claim mechanics, before the 2021 boom.

#### Digital Zones of Immaterial Pictorial Sensibility — Mitchell F. Chan (2017)
- Type: Artwork · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Burn & destruction, Contract & certificate, Supply & scarcity
- Idea: Put an artist's sale ritual in a contract: buyers must deal with the contract directly, and 'owning the immaterial' means burning the receipt.
- What it is: An ERC-20 token work deployed on 29 August 2017 and unveiled at InterAccess, Toronto. It restages Yves Klein's Zones of Immaterial Pictorial Sensibility, including a ritual any holder can perform to destroy their token.
- Protocol: IKB is sold only by calling the contract's buy() function (no front end, on purpose). Supply is capped at 101 tokens in 8 series: Series 0 has 31 tokens at 0.1 ETH, each later series 10 tokens at double the previous price, with later series meant to be released over the artist's lifetime. Every sale is recorded per edition. A holder may call ritual(): the token is burned and half the ETH paid for it is sent to a separate contract called theRiver, echoing Klein throwing half the gold into the Seine.
- What the collective did: All Series 0 tokens sold out (artist's README, 2021); buyers had to work out how to call the contract by hand. The contract records each edition's buyer, price and whether it has been ritually burned.

## Autonomous Entities & DAOs

Artworks that own themselves, reproduce, or are governed by their community: self-owning forests, blockchain life forms, autonomous artists and art DAOs.

### DAOs & Collective Governance

Art projects where token holders vote, fund and decide: daily auctions, artist careers run by shareholders, collective purchases.

#### Clock — Pak (2022)
- Type: Artwork · Substrate: Ethereum & L2s · Mechanisms: Time, decay & death, Voting & governance
- Idea: A 1/1 whose only content is a running count of a political fact, sold so that a crowd had to organise to own it.
- What it is: The single-edition token of Censored: a counter of the days Julian Assange had spent in prison, updated daily. It was auctioned in February 2022 and bought by AssangeDAO, a crowd-funded collective.
- Protocol: Token #1 of the Censored contract renders the number of days of Assange's imprisonment and changes every day; it was sold at auction to fund Assange's legal defence. The clock was set to stop on his release.
- What the collective did: AssangeDAO pooled ETH from more than 10,000 contributors through Juicebox (as reported by the DAO and press) and won the auction with 16,593 ETH, about $52.8 million at the time. The token now sits in a multisig safe; the clock stopped when Assange was freed in June 2024.

## Theory & Texts

Books, essays and research programmes that define protocols, systems and participation in art.

### Systems & Protocol Theory

Texts on systems aesthetics, protocols and control in networks.

#### Paragraphs on Conceptual Art — Sol LeWitt (1967)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Instruction & score, Rules & constraints
- Idea: Write the plan first; once it exists, the idea becomes a machine that makes the art.
- What it is: Essay published in the Summer 1967 issue of Artforum in which LeWitt defines Conceptual art: the idea is the most important part of the work, and its execution follows a plan fixed in advance.
- Protocol: All planning and decisions are made beforehand and 'the execution is a perfunctory affair'; the artist fixes a premise and a system, and anyone following it produces the work without new aesthetic choices.
- What the collective did: The text became the working manifesto for instruction-based practice: LeWitt's own wall drawings are still executed by drafters and museum teams from his written instructions, and later instruction, score and code-based artists cite it as a starting point.
- Text: https://www.artforum.com/features/paragraphs-on-conceptual-art-211354/ (Artforum 5, no. 10 (Summer 1967))
- Images: https://www.artforum.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/article00_large-51.jpg
- Project page: https://www.artforum.com/features/paragraphs-on-conceptual-art-211354/

#### Systems Esthetics — Jack Burnham (1968)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Rules & constraints, Open participation
- Idea: The artwork is not the object but the system of relations it sets in motion.
- What it is: Essay in Artforum (September 1968) arguing that art was shifting from making objects to organising systems: relations between people, materials, information and institutions over time.
- Protocol: Burnham borrows from systems theory and cybernetics: a work is a set of components and the rules of their interaction, and the artist designs those relations instead of a finished form.
- What the collective did: The essay gave critics and artists (Hans Haacke among them) a language for process, ecological and information-based work, and is now a standard reference for software, network and protocol art.
- Text: https://www.artforum.com/features/systems-esthetics-201372/ (Artforum 7, no. 1 (September 1968), pp. 30–35)
- Images: https://www.artforum.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/article00_large-23.jpg
- Project page: https://www.artforum.com/features/systems-esthetics-201372/

#### Sentences on Conceptual Art — Sol LeWitt (1969)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Instruction & score, Rules & constraints
- Idea: Once the process is set, let it run: follow the rules even when the result surprises you.
- What it is: Thirty-five numbered statements first printed in the magazine 0 to 9 (no. 5, January 1969) and then in the first issue of Art-Language (May 1969), extending LeWitt's argument that ideas alone can be works of art.
- Protocol: The sentences state that the artist should not change the idea mid-course, that the process is mechanical once begun, and that 'the idea becomes a machine that makes the art'; perception of the work is left to each viewer.
- What the collective did: Reprinted in most Conceptual art anthologies, the sentences are read as a rulebook by artists who write systems for others to run, from wall-drawing assistants to generative and smart-contract artists.
- Text: https://monoskop.org/images/3/3d/LeWitt_Sol_1969_1999_Sentences_on_Conceptual_Art.pdf (0 to 9, no. 5 (January 1969); Art-Language 1, no. 1 (May 1969))
- Images: https://wiels.org/uploads/resized/uploads/355769/Sol-LeWitts-Sentences-on-Conceptual-Art-Manuscript-and-Draft-Material-196869_58f02840803f2ddfa28c8dbfb30a044c.jpg
- Project page: https://wiels.org/en/events/sol-lewitts-sentences-on-conceptual-art-manuscript-and-draft-material-1968-69

#### Software: Information Technology: Its New Meaning for Art — Jack Burnham (1970)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Instruction & score, Open participation
- Idea: An exhibition can be a program: instructions that visitors and machines execute together.
- What it is: Exhibition and catalogue curated by Burnham at the Jewish Museum, New York (16 September – 8 November 1970), that showed conceptual artists and computer systems side by side, treating 'software' as instructions carried out by machines or by visitors.
- Protocol: Works were framed as 'software': sets of instructions written by the artist and run by a computer or by the public, such as Hans Haacke's Visitor's Profile, which tabulated visitors' answers to a questionnaire, and Ted Nelson's hypertext catalogue Labyrinth.
- What the collective did: Visitors' answers and actions supplied the content of several works; the show, which also travelled to the Smithsonian, is remembered as the first major US museum exhibition to bring the computer into the gallery.
- Text: https://monoskop.org/Software_(exhibition) (The Jewish Museum, New York, 1970 (exhibition catalogue))
- Images: https://monoskop.org/images/5/5b/Software-catalogue.jpg
- Project page: https://www.fondation-langlois.org/html/e/page.php?NumPage=541

#### Conceptual Art 1962–1969: From the Aesthetic of Administration to the Critique of Institutions — Benjamin H. D. Buchloh (1990)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Contract & certificate, Instruction & score
- Idea: Paperwork is a medium: certificates and contracts can carry the whole work.
- What it is: Essay in October (Winter 1990) that reads Conceptual art through its use of administrative forms: certificates, statements, lists, legal contracts and instructions.
- Protocol: Buchloh argues that artists such as LeWitt, Kosuth, Weiner and Haacke replaced craft with the procedures of bureaucracy, so that the work consists of rules, documents and institutional relations.
- What the collective did: The essay framed later readings of certificates, receipts and artist contracts as art, the lineage that curators and writers now connect to smart-contract art.
- Text: https://www.jstor.org/stable/778941 (October 55 (Winter 1990), MIT Press)
- Project page: https://doi.org/10.2307/778941

#### Is There Love in the Telematic Embrace? — Roy Ascott (1990)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Open participation, Co-authorship & derivatives, Circulation & passing on
- Idea: On a network, authorship is distributed: the artist sets up the link, the participants make the work.
- What it is: Essay in Art Journal (Autumn 1990) in which Ascott describes telematic art: works made by many participants linked through computer networks, with meaning produced in their exchanges.
- Protocol: The artist provides a networked context and a few rules for contribution; participants at distant nodes add, transform and pass on material, and no single author controls the result.
- What the collective did: The essay draws on Ascott's own networked projects, such as La Plissure du Texte (1983), in which participants in several cities wrote a fairy tale together; it became a founding text for telematic and net art and titles his collected writings, Telematic Embrace (2003).
- Text: https://www.jstor.org/stable/777114 (Art Journal 49, no. 3 (Autumn 1990), pp. 241–247)
- Images: https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780520222946-L.jpg
- Project page: https://doi.org/10.1080/00043249.1990.10792697

#### Protocol: How Control Exists after Decentralization — Alexander R. Galloway (2004)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Rules & constraints, Voting & governance
- Idea: Protocol is how control works when there is no centre: design the protocol and you design the behaviour.
- What it is: Book (MIT Press, 2004) arguing that the internet is organised not by freedom but by protocols such as TCP/IP and DNS, which distribute control into technical standards; it also discusses net art and hacking as practices that work inside protocol.
- Protocol: Galloway defines protocol as a set of voluntary technical rules that every node must follow to connect; its power lies in making behaviour standard while leaving each node apparently free.
- What the collective did: The book became the main theory text for net art, tactical media and later crypto and protocol art, and is cited by artists who treat standards and smart contracts as their medium.
- Text: https://direct.mit.edu/books/book/2528/ProtocolHow-Control-Exists-after-Decentralization (MIT Press, Leonardo Book Series, 2004)
- Images: https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/isbn/9780262072472-L.jpg
- Project page: https://direct.mit.edu/books/book/2528/ProtocolHow-Control-Exists-after-Decentralization

#### Words Made Flesh: Code, Culture, Imagination — Florian Cramer (2005)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Instruction & score, Rules & constraints
- Idea: A score, a spell and a program share one property: words that make things happen when executed.
- What it is: Book-length essay written at the Piet Zwart Institute in Rotterdam and published online in 2005, tracing the idea of executable language from magic and combinatory poetry to Fluxus scores and computer code.
- Protocol: Cramer shows that instructions have a long cultural history outside computing, and that the same text can be read as description or run as action, by people or by machines.
- What the collective did: Distributed free online and through the Internet Archive, the text became a common reference in software-art and code-poetry teaching.
- Text: https://archive.org/details/WordsMadeFlesh (Piet Zwart Institute, Rotterdam, 2005)
- Images: https://archive.org/services/img/WordsMadeFlesh
- Project page: https://www.netzliteratur.net/cramer/wordsmadefleshpdf.pdf

#### The Exploit: A Theory of Networks — Alexander R. Galloway, Eugene Thacker (2007)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Rules & constraints, Voting & governance
- Idea: To change a network, use its own rules against it.
- What it is: Book (University of Minnesota Press, 2007) arguing that networks create their own forms of control, both centralised and dispersed, and that resistance must work through 'exploits': holes found inside the protocol itself.
- Protocol: The authors describe networks as topologies governed by protocol, and the exploit as a practice that follows the rules exactly while turning them to unintended ends.
- What the collective did: The book gave hacktivists, tactical-media groups and software artists a vocabulary for working inside network protocols rather than outside them.
- Text: https://www.upress.umn.edu/book-division/books/the-exploit (University of Minnesota Press, Electronic Mediations 21, 2007)

- Project page: https://www.upress.umn.edu/book-division/books/the-exploit

#### As for Protocols — Vera List Center for Art and Politics (2025)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Rules & constraints, Open participation
- Idea: Protocols are social forms of care and relation, and artists can write more equitable ones.
- What it is: Open-access book edited by Re'al Christian, Carin Kuoni and Eriola Pira (Vera List Center for Art and Politics and Amherst College Press, 2025) with contributions by 24 artists, writers, scholars, musicians, architects and scientists on protocols.
- Protocol: The book frames protocols as 'systems of language that regulate how we relate to each other' and to technologies, and asks contributors to propose new protocols that are inclusive and equitable.
- What the collective did: Contributors include Raven Chacon, Carolina Caycedo, Mary Maggic, Shannon Mattern, Rasheedah Phillips, Leanne Betasamosake Simpson, Ultra-red and Underground Resistance, drawn from the Center's multi-year focus theme.
- Text: https://www.fulcrum.org/concern/monographs/3t945t729 (Vera List Center for Art and Politics / Amherst College Press, 2025)

### Participation & Relational Art

Texts on participatory, relational and social practice art.

#### Relational Aesthetics — Nicolas Bourriaud (1998)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Open participation, Market & exchange
- Idea: The artwork can be a situation: a set of conditions under which people meet and interact.
- What it is: Book first published in French as Esthétique relationnelle (Les presses du réel, 1998; English 2002) that describes 1990s art whose material is human relations: shared meals, meetings, services and temporary social spaces.
- Protocol: Bourriaud describes artworks as 'social interstices': the artist sets up a situation with loose rules of use, and visitors complete it by how they gather, eat, talk or exchange.
- What the collective did: The book named a generation (Rirkrit Tiravanija, Liam Gillick, Philippe Parreno and others) and became one of the most cited and most debated texts on participatory art.
- Text: https://www.lespressesdureel.com/EN/ouvrage.php?id=12797&menu=0 (Les presses du réel, Dijon, 1998)

#### Conversation Pieces: Community and Communication in Modern Art — Grant Kester (2004)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Open participation, Co-authorship & derivatives
- Idea: Design the conversation, not the object: the work is what people say and decide together.
- What it is: Book (University of California Press, 2004) on 'dialogical' art, where artists set up long-term exchanges with communities and the conversation itself becomes the work.
- Protocol: Kester describes projects in which the artist frames a dialogue among groups that rarely meet (for example officials, workers and residents) and the outcome is decided in that exchange.
- What the collective did: The book, reissued in an updated edition in 2013, became a key reference for collaborative and community-based art, offering an alternative to judging such work by its visual form.

#### Participation — Claire Bishop (2006)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Open participation, Instruction & score
- Idea: Participation has a history and a theory: read the rules artists wrote for audiences side by side.
- What it is: Anthology edited by Bishop (Whitechapel Gallery and MIT Press, Documents of Contemporary Art, 2006) that collects theory, artists' statements and curatorial debates on art that makes spectators into participants.
- Protocol: The book gathers texts in which artists define what participants may do, from Lygia Clark, Hélio Oiticica and Joseph Beuys to Thomas Hirschhorn and Rirkrit Tiravanija, next to theory by Eco, Barthes, Rancière and Guattari.
- What the collective did: It became a standard course reader on participatory art and included the first English translation of Rancière's 'Problems and Transformations in Critical Art'.

#### Artificial Hells: Participatory Art and the Politics of Spectatorship — Claire Bishop (2012)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Open participation, Rules & constraints
- Idea: Judge a participatory work by the tensions its rules create, not only by how nice the collaboration feels.
- What it is: Book (Verso, 2012) that gives the first historical and theoretical overview of socially engaged participatory art, from Futurist evenings and Situationism to 1960s happenings and recent social practice.
- Protocol: Bishop analyses how artists 'delegate' performance and set rules for participants, and argues against judging such work only by its ethics; the best works keep a productive discomfort between author and participants.
- What the collective did: The book shaped a decade of debate on social practice and is widely taught; its account of delegated performance applies directly to works where crowds act under an artist's rules.

#### do it: the compendium — Hans Ulrich Obrist (2013)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Instruction & score, Open participation, Co-authorship & derivatives
- Idea: An exhibition can travel as a set of instructions and be remade differently each time.
- What it is: Book (Independent Curators International and D.A.P., 2013) collecting twenty years of the do it exhibition, begun in Paris in 1993, in which artists write instructions that institutions and readers carry out.

- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Contract & certificate, Rules & constraints, Time, decay & death
- Idea: The blockchain is not a channel for images but the material itself: a set of constraints adopted for art.
- What it is: Essay written for World Computer Sculpture Garden, a 'contract show' curated by 0xfff that opened on Ethereum in November 2024, defining protocol art as art that works with the rules that precede and define a computing environment.
- Protocol: Rauch argues that protocol artists treat distributed computation and its permanence as their medium, writing contracts that act as autonomous works rather than minting images for a marketplace.
- What the collective did: The essay accompanies an exhibition that exists as a smart contract with no closing date, with works by artists including Rhea Myers and Sarah Friend, and is cited in academic work on protocol art.

#### Protocol Art — Mat Dryhurst (2025)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Voting & governance, Co-authorship & derivatives, Rules & constraints
- Idea: Build the protocol as 'executable critique': a rule set others can use that carries your values downstream.
- What it is: Lecture published by Carrier Bag in October 2025 in which Dryhurst argues that the most consequential aesthetic decisions now happen at the level of protocols, and that artists should design protocols instead of commenting on platforms.
- Protocol: Dryhurst uses his and Holly Herndon's projects as examples: Holly+, which lets anyone use Herndon's voice model under shared rules, the opt-out tool haveibeentrained.com, and Public Diffusion, a model trained only on public-domain data.
- What the collective did: The talk extends the protocol-art discussion from smart contracts to AI: the work is judged by what other people make and decide under the rules it sets.

#### Protocol Futuring: Speculating Second-Order Dynamics of Protocols in Sociotechnical Infrastructural Futures — Botao Amber Hu (2025)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Rules & constraints, Time, decay & death, Co-authorship & derivatives
- Idea: Speculate on protocols, not products: watch how rules drift when they pass between groups and generations.
- What it is: CHI 2026 paper by Botao Amber Hu, Samuel Chua and Helena Rong that proposes 'protocol futuring', a design method that uses protocols as the material of speculative design, tested in a relay workshop on millennial-scale knowledge preservation.
- Protocol: In the Knowledge Futurama workshop, teams inherited partially formed protocol designs from earlier teams and had to reinterpret and extend them, a relay rule that made drift, jamming and adversarial readings visible.
- What the collective did: The multi-team relay produced protocols that changed through ambiguous handovers, shifting norms and simulated crises; the paper offers the method to HCI and design researchers studying long-lived infrastructures.
- Text: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06108 (CHI 2026)
- Project page: https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790527

#### Protocol as Poetry: A Case Study of Pak's Smart Contract-Based Protocol Art — Botao Amber Hu (2025)
- Type: Book & essay · Substrate: Text & paper · Mechanisms: Contract & certificate, Open participation, Co-authorship & derivatives
- Idea: In protocol art the smart contract is the medium and collective participation is the message.
- What it is: Peer-reviewed paper (ARTECH 2025) that analyses Pak's smart-contract works, from The Fungible and Merge (2021) to Censored (2022) and Not Found (2023), and proposes seven characteristics of protocol art.
- Protocol: The paper names seven traits: system-centric composition, autonomous governance, distributed agency and communal authorship, temporal dynamism, economy-driven engagement, poetic messages embedded in interaction rituals, and composability.
- What the collective did: It is one of the first academic studies to use the term 'protocol art' (or 'protocolism') for smart-contract works and distinguishes it from conceptual, generative, participatory and performance art.
- Text: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12393 (ARTECH 2025, 12th International Conference on Digital and Interactive Arts)
- Project page: https://doi.org/10.1145/3773699.3773918
